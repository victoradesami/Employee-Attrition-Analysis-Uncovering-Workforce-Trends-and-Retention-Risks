# Employee-Attrition-Analysis-Uncovering-Workforce-Trends-and-Retention-Risks

## Key insights from the dashboard

# Employee attrition is a significant concern
- The organization has approximately 4,700 employees, with 1,359 employees having left.
- The overall attrition rate is approximately 29%, while the retention rate is around 71%.
- This indicates that employee turnover is a significant workforce management concern that may affect recruitment costs, productivity, and organizational stability.
Insight: The organization should investigate the factors driving employee departures and develop targeted retention strategies.

# HR has the highest departmental attrition rate
The dashboard shows the following attrition rates by department:
Department	Attrition rate
HR	33%
Marketing	32%
Sales	29%
Engineering	28%
Finance	25%
- HR records the highest attrition rate at 33%, followed by Marketing at 32%.
- Finance has the lowest rate at 25%.
Insight: HR and Marketing may be experiencing greater employee retention challenges than other departments. Further investigation into workload, management practices, career progression, and compensation could help explain the differences.

# Sales-related roles account for the highest number of departures
The leading job roles by employee departures include:
- Sales Manager: 207 employees
- Sales Executive: 184 employees
- Software Engineer: 142 employees
- Tech Lead: 120 employees
- QA Engineer: 118 employees
Insight: Sales Managers and Sales Executives account for the largest numbers of departures among the roles displayed. This could affect customer relationships, sales continuity, and revenue generation.
However, these are departure counts, not role-specific attrition rates. Roles with more employees may naturally have more departures, so headcount-adjusted rates should be calculated before comparing risk across roles.

# Employees with shorter tenure appear to be at greater risk
The tenure analysis indicates that the 0–1 year group has the highest attrition rate, at approximately 40%. The rate declines across the longer-tenure groups, reaching roughly 27% for employees with 10 or more years of service.
Insight: Early-tenure employees appear particularly vulnerable to leaving. This may indicate challenges with onboarding, job expectations, training, workload, or the employee experience during the first year.
The organization should pay particular attention to the first 12 months of employment.

# Overtime is associated with higher attrition
- Employees who work overtime have an attrition rate of approximately 41%.
- Employees who do not work overtime have a rate of approximately 23%.
Insight: Employees working overtime appear substantially more likely to leave. This may reflect workload pressure, work-life balance challenges, or insufficient staffing.
The relationship is an association, not proof that overtime directly causes employees to leave.

# Lower-income employees show higher attrition
The dashboard indicates the following pattern:
- Below 45K: approximately 43%
- 45K–60K: approximately 24%
- 60K–80K: approximately 28%
- Above 80K: approximately 26%
Insight: The lowest income band has the highest attrition rate. This may suggest that compensation, perceived fairness, or opportunities for financial advancement are worth investigating.
The organization should compare pay against role responsibilities, performance, internal salary structures, and market benchmarks before deciding on compensation changes.

# Job satisfaction is associated with employee departures
The dashboard shows attrition rates of approximately:
- Satisfaction level 1: 35%
- Satisfaction level 2: 37%
- Satisfaction level 3: 23%
- Satisfaction level 4: 22%
Insight: Employees in the lower satisfaction categories have higher attrition rates than those in the higher categories. Improving the employee experience and addressing workplace concerns may therefore support retention.
The satisfaction scale should be confirmed against the dataset's definitions before interpreting the categories.

# Distance from work may be a contributing factor
- Employees living more than 21 km away have an attrition rate of approximately 37%.
- Other distance groups generally show lower rates, around 25–27%.
Insight: Longer commutes may contribute to dissatisfaction, fatigue, or work-life balance difficulties. Flexible working arrangements or transport support may be worth evaluating where operationally feasible.

# Performance ratings and age show smaller differences
- Employees with performance rating 2 have the highest displayed attrition rate, at approximately 33%.
- Employees with rating 4 have the lowest, at approximately 21%.
- Attrition across age groups is relatively close, ranging from approximately 28% to 30%.
Insight: Performance ratings may help identify groups for further investigation, but the dashboard does not establish why employees leave. Age differences appear relatively small, so age should not be treated as a primary driver based on these results alone.

## Overall business recommendations

# Introduce a structured employee retention program
Prioritize employees in their first year, particularly those in high-turnover roles and departments. Conduct 30-, 60-, and 90-day check-ins, provide onboarding support, and establish mentoring programs to identify concerns before employees decide to leave.


# Review overtime and workload distribution
Examine overtime patterns by department, job role, manager, and employee tenure. Improve staffing plans, redistribute workloads, and monitor excessive overtime to reduce burnout and improve work-life balance.


# Review compensation and career progression
Assess whether salaries are competitive and equitable, particularly for employees in the lowest income band. Communicate promotion criteria, provide professional development opportunities, and create clear career paths.


# Improve employee satisfaction and management support
Use anonymous employee surveys, exit interviews, and stay interviews to identify concerns involving leadership, recognition, workload, team relationships, and career opportunities. Assign responsibility for addressing recurring issues.


# Develop targeted departmental retention strategies
Investigate HR and Marketing, which have the highest displayed departmental attrition rates. Review turnover by job role and employee headcount, then work with department managers to address the specific challenges affecting each team.


# Monitor attrition through regular HR reporting
Track monthly attrition, retention, voluntary departures, first-year turnover, overtime exposure, and departmental attrition rates. Compare results before and after retention initiatives to assess whether the interventions are effective.

## Executive Summary: Employee Attrition Analysis

The HR Analytics Dashboard was developed using Power BI to examine employee attrition patterns and identify workforce characteristics associated with employee departures. The dashboard analyzes 4,700 employees and reports 1,359 departures, an overall attrition rate of approximately 29%, and a retention rate of around 71%.


The analysis reveals several notable patterns. HR and Marketing have the highest departmental attrition rates, at 33% and 32%, respectively. Sales Managers and Sales Executives record the highest departure counts among the displayed job roles. Employees with shorter tenure, those working overtime, and those in the lowest income band also show comparatively high attrition rates. In addition, lower job satisfaction and longer commuting distances are associated with higher employee turnover in the displayed data.

These findings suggest that employee attrition should be addressed through targeted retention initiatives rather than a single organization-wide intervention. Recommended actions include strengthening onboarding, reviewing workload and overtime practices, assessing compensation competitiveness, improving career development opportunities, and investigating department-specific challenges.

Overall, the dashboard demonstrates how data analytics can help HR teams identify workforce risks, prioritize retention efforts, and monitor the effectiveness of employee engagement strategies. The observed relationships should be investigated further before drawing conclusions about causation.
